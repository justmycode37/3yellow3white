Better explanations with shorter prompts

Research-led revision of guidance.md and story-review.md, tested on actual narration and per-scene explanation/visualization plans.

No video, animation, or audio was generated. This evaluates scripts and planned evidence, not rendered execution or student learning.

What the transcript research supports

The prior study fully read 117 substantive English explainers across 2015–2024: 355,560 words and approximately 32.4 caption-span hours. It inventoried 153 files, of which 150 were nonempty; 33 other-format videos were outside the close-reading set. Sources are creator-owned captions at historical repository commit 0bce59666e3b16b449d414da253beb11257b5da6. Captions were read, not videos watched; timestamps are approximate and caption defects limit visual/timing claims. This is a broad historical corpus, not all videos through October 2026. Full 117-video study and source index.

The transferable pattern is guided reasoning: establish a question, teach a useful representation, perform or compare something, inspect its consequence, then generalize with limits. This is a local reasoning pattern, not a mandatory whole-video template. Existing prompts already contained much of it. Their largest differences were rigid local prescriptions, repeated advice, and weaker tests of whether the visual plan itself supplies usable evidence.

Structure follows the obstacle, not a universal opening sequence.

2015/music-and-measure-theory, 07:06–11:22: An analytic proof arrives first; the explanation then acknowledges that the proof has not repaired intuition and develops a geometric encoding.

Prompt gap: Concrete-before-abstract opening and skip-definitions rules were stronger than the broader adapt-to-topic instruction.

Change: Allow a definition, known result, investigation, or worked example to lead; choose the sequence that earns understanding for this audience.

A formula can be an early object of investigation, not only the final reveal.

2021/quick-eigen, 00:14–03:12: Prior-knowledge shortcut lesson reviews mathematical facts and asks viewers to rediscover the trick; exact calculation is central.

Prompt gap: The example→need→idea→name→symbol→formula ladder can bias every topic toward the same order.

Change: Move between cases, qualitative reasoning and formalism as needed; explain the consequential relationship regardless of when notation appears.

Visual operations implement the inference.

2019/bayes-theorem, 03:24–10:13: Selected population subsets change the denominator; the same conditioning operation is then represented with relative areas.

Prompt gap: Visuals were requested, but author guidance emphasized reasoning in spoken sentences more explicitly than a testable visual argument.

Change: Specify encoding, operation/comparison, changed and fixed quantities, and the observation supporting the inference. Speech directs attention and explains significance.

Teach each representation, then map between representations.

2018/fourier-transforms, 02:40–10:35; 12:32–18:26: Signal, wound graph and center-of-mass plot are defined and recapped; the formula later reconstructs rotation and averaging operations, including normalization.

Prompt gap: Meaning-before-notation was useful but did not explicitly require teaching visual encodings and grounding deictic words such as this.

Change: Identify referents and teach unfamiliar axes/marks before using them as evidence; a perspective shift maps the old objects to the new view.

Pacing follows novelty and dependency.

2020/hamming-codes, 08:54–11:10; 11:11–11:41: Column checks receive a long first explanation; analogous row checks then compress substantially.

Prompt gap: Opening 20–30 words and later scenes 30–60 words conflict with many requested scene-count/duration combinations; one new demand per beat conflates scene with cognitive step.

Change: Use coherent reasoning units containing linked steps, budget total speech, spend time on the first unfamiliar operation and compress established repetition.

Continuity preserves meaning; morphing is optional and sometimes misleading.

2016/nonsquare-matrices, 01:00–01:21; 03:26–03:42: Side-by-side input and output spaces deliberately avoid implying that 2D and 3D vectors inhabit the same space; dots replace grid imagery when outputs are numbers.

Prompt gap: Strong downstream continuity requirements could be mistaken for a pedagogical requirement that every change be continuously morphed.

Change: Preserve relevant identity and correspondence; permit static comparisons, distinct views and explicit transitions.

Reasonable failures motivate new tools; they are not compulsory drama.

2020/better-bayes, 08:43–14:41: A useful small-prior approximation gives an impossible prediction outside its range, motivating exact odds updates.

Prompt gap: Existing failed-attempt guidance was already good; adding more story devices would duplicate it.

Change: Retain one concise optional failed-attempt instruction and reusable reasoning moves; remove quotas and repeated explanations of the same principle.

A compelling picture still needs model boundaries and a valid argument.

2023/convolutions2, 15:26; 23:29–24:44: The explanation distinguishes the input and output parameters, synchronizes two views with the same parameter, and corrects the geometric slice's square-root-of-two scale factor.

Prompt gap: Accuracy checks existed, but reviewing facts and continuity alone could accept a conclusion merely restated by a diagram.

Change: Review the actual inferential bridge and visual evidence. Reject material gaps or misleading encodings; do not reject unconventional but sound structure.

Evaluation design and limitations

Five requests cover Bayes, plant gas exchange, idempotency keys, uniform convergence, and historical source interpretation. Each has one fresh-context generation under original, reduced-guidance control, and candidate prompts: 15 drafts, not five repeated runs of each scenario. The control retains format, syntax example/final checklist, planning contract, renderer capabilities, and visual policy; it removes the main narrative guidance. It is not an instruction-free control.

Two fresh evaluators scored anonymized A/B/C packets without prompt labels, using a rubric recorded before revision. Seven dimensions cover audience/scope, inference, representation, accuracy/limits, pacing, participation, and handoff. Scores and severities remain fallible model judgments. No statistical superiority or human learning benefit is established; several original and control drafts were already strong. The comparison evaluates prompt packages, not isolated effects of individual sentences.

Generation used native subagents with assembled application prompts. The application's subscription runner reported no configured credential, so these are not production-provider end-to-end runs. The application parsers and review validators were used locally. First-pass outputs are preserved, including errors; no silent repairs inflate pass rates.

Actual drafts: format and timing

Planning estimate: (60 × spoken words / words per minute + scripted silence) × 1.1. Central estimate uses 140 wpm; range uses 150–125. No acoustic timestamps were measured. Requested runtimes: Bayes/protocol/interactive 180s, leaf 120s, uniform 240s, history 90s. The retained source-name failures use a harness request filename where the contract requires literal request; this confound is separate from teaching quality and cannot be credited as a proven narrative-prompt effect.
