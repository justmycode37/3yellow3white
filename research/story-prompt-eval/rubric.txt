Script-and-plan evaluation only. No claim about rendered animation, spoken delivery, retention, or production inference quality.

Read each script and its complete scene plan against its exact request. Rate each dimension 0 (missing/wrong), 1 (material weakness), 2 (usable but clear improvement needed), 3 (strong). Record concrete passage/scene evidence; don't reward preferred wording or narrative format.
1. Audience and scope: requested knowledge and coverage, not substituted simpler topic.
2. Explanatory bridge: viewer can reconstruct why conclusion follows; appropriate causal mechanism, deductive argument, interpretation, or comparison.
3. Representation: stated encoding, observable relation/operation, speech-to-visual referents; evidence for inference rather than labels repeating it.
4. Accuracy and boundaries: calculations, assumptions, analogies, limits, uncertainty, source fidelity.
5. Progression and pacing: dependencies, attention, first operation vs repetition, duration and scene constraints without padding or fragmentation.
6. Viewer reasoning: purposeful reachable tasks when useful/requested; complete default explanation; requested pause policy and reveal order.
7. Handoff: semantic identity, compatible adjacent states, useful per-scene instructions without rigid layout/API choreography.

Separately list material errors and nonblocking improvements. Good scripts can start with a definition, show a formula early, have no question/pause, use multiple linked operations in one scene, or remain nonmathematical. Judge the request and inference, not those choices alone.
Estimated duration uses (60 * spoken_words / words_per_minute + explicit_pause_seconds) * 1.1, with 125–150 wpm as a planning range. This is not measured audio. Flag major under/overruns with evidence; do not prefer padding to adequate explanation.

Paired samples are exploratory, one output per topic per prompt. Different topics are not repeat runs of an identical scenario. Scores do not establish statistical superiority.
