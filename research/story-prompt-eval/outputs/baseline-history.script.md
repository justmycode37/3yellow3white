# Can one source tell us why workers protested?

## Beat 1 — A report and a possible motive

Content needed: Fictional source packet. A dated evidence timeline distinguishes the 4 May notice from its announced 10 May wage-cut start, and the 11 May diary from the events it reports. Show the diary quotation exactly: My pay will not cover rent now. Keep the interpretation separate from source wording. Preserve notice and diary identities for beat-2.

Narration: On 11 May, a worker writes that several workers have stopped work. Was a wage cut the reason? Our fictional packet offers clues.

Narration: A factory notice dated 4 May announces a ten percent wage cut starting on 10 May. The next day's diary reports the stoppage and says, "My pay will not cover rent now." This directly records the diarist's concern about rent. The timing links that concern with the announced cut, supporting financial pressure as a possible motive. But the diary does not say why every worker stopped.

## Beat 2 — Weigh the claim, limit the conclusion

Content needed: Continue with the same evidence. Add the 12 May manager letter attributing the stoppage to outside agitators, with none identified. Compare what each source reports with what it supports. Reveal the qualified interpretation only with the final spoken block. No other worker interviews, attendance totals, earlier wage records, or additional evidence are supplied.

Narration: A manager's letter, dated 12 May, blames outside agitators but identifies none. It directly shows what the manager claimed; it does not establish that agitators caused the stoppage. Both writers offer partial views. We should neither dismiss the manager's claim nor turn one worker's worry into everyone's motive.

Reveal (spoken): The wage explanation has more specific support: an announced cut, a reported stoppage soon afterward, and one worker's concern about rent. The cut may have contributed to the stoppage. Without other workers' accounts or further evidence, we cannot establish a single cause or all workers' motives. A reported claim and a justified conclusion are different things.
