# Can one source tell us why workers protested?

## Beat 1 — What the sources actually say

Content needed: All sources and events are fictional. Show the factory notice dated 4 May, its announced 10% wage cut starting 10 May, and the worker diary dated 11 May. A chronological line separates document dates from the announced start date. Show the exact diary excerpt, then distinguish its report of several workers stopping work from the writer's own financial concern. Do not depict a known crowd size or invent a date for the stoppage.

Narration: These fictional sources describe a factory dispute. What can they tell us about why workers stopped work? A notice dated the fourth of May announces a ten percent wage cut, starting on the tenth. A diary dated the eleventh reports that several workers stopped work. Its writer adds, “My pay will not cover rent now.” The notice establishes what the factory announced. The diary reports a stoppage and directly expresses this writer's concern about rent. Together, they suggest a connection between the wage cut and the dispute. But one worker's words cannot establish what motivated every worker.

## Beat 2 — Weigh a competing explanation

Content needed: Retain the notice and diary as evidence; add the manager letter dated 12 May. Its attribution to outside agitators must be labeled as the manager's claim, with no named agitators. Compare the specific concern expressed in the diary with the unsupported identification of a cause in the letter. Reveal a qualified interpretation only after that comparison. Supplied gaps: no interviews with other workers, attendance totals, earlier wage records, or additional evidence.

Narration: A manager's letter dated the twelfth offers another explanation: outside agitators. Yet the manager identifies none. The letter is evidence that the manager made this claim; it does not establish that agitators caused the stoppage. Compare the support: the diary gives a specific financial concern, while the letter names no agitators. Our interpretation can therefore be qualified: the announced wage cut plausibly contributed to the stoppage, and rent was a concern for at least this writer. Other motives remain possible. Without other workers' accounts or further evidence, we cannot say how widely that concern was shared, or settle the cause.
