# Can one source tell us why workers protested?

## Beat 1 — What the sources actually say

Content needed: All material is fictional. Build a dated evidence sequence: notice dated 4 May announcing a 10% wage cut starting 10 May; diary dated 11 May reporting that several workers stopped work and stating, ‘My pay will not cover rent now’; manager letter dated 12 May attributing the stoppage to unidentified outside agitators. Distinguish the date of the diary from an independently established date of the stoppage. Keep all claims attached to their sources.

Narration: Why did these factory workers stop work? Our fictional packet contains three sources, but they do different jobs. A notice dated May fourth announces a ten percent wage cut, starting May tenth. That establishes an announced policy; it does not show anyone’s actual pay.

Narration: A diary dated May eleventh reports that several workers stopped work. Its writer says, ‘My pay will not cover rent now.’ We have a report of a stoppage and one person’s financial concern. A manager’s letter dated May twelfth blames outside agitators, but names none. That records the manager’s explanation; it does not establish that agitators caused the stoppage.

## Beat 2 — From reports to a qualified interpretation

Content needed: Compare what each source supplies with the inference it can support. Preserve the notice’s status as an announcement, the diary’s single-writer scope, and the manager’s unsubstantiated attribution. Reach a qualified wage-related interpretation without treating chronology as proof. Show the limits: no other worker interviews, attendance totals, earlier wage records, or additional evidence.

Narration: The wage explanation has some support: the cut was due to begin before the diary entry, and the writer connects pay with difficulty covering rent. Together, these make financial pressure a plausible contributor to the stoppage. But the diary does not explicitly say why every participant joined, or even that its writer joined.

Narration: The manager offers a competing explanation. Naming no agitators leaves that claim unsupported within this packet, rather than proven false. Without other workers’ accounts, attendance totals, or earlier wage records, we cannot settle everyone’s motives. Our conclusion is qualified: the announced cut may have contributed to the stoppage, while the reasons individual workers took part remain uncertain.
